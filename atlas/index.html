India's
Bump Atlas

Right now every bump gets a sound effect. Next, every bump becomes a data point.

With your permission, Gandkari Detector will note where each bump happened. Put enough drives together, separate real potholes from someone dropping their phone, and the result is a living map of the country's speed breakers, potholes and surprise craters: which road, which stretch, and how hard it hits.

How it will work

  • Opt-in only. Location stays off unless you switch it on, and is only used while a session is running.
  • Many drivers, one map. A single bump means little. The same spot hit by many cars, at different speeds, on different phones, becomes a confident pin.
  • Fakes get filtered. Shaking a phone at a red light does not look like a road. Signal checks and agreement between drivers decide what makes the map.
  • Severity is fair. Each bump is rated after accounting for speed and phone model, on the jhatka scale from halka jhatka to suspension ka antim sanskar.
  • Roads, not people. Only aggregated road segments are shown. No individual trips, no accounts, and the start and end of every drive are trimmed so homes and offices never appear.
  • A map everyone can see. Drivers know what's coming, and anyone can see exactly which roads need attention.

What it will never do

  • Ask for a name, email or phone number.
  • Show where any one person drove.
  • Record audio or use the camera.
